How to get Quantitative Research roles in Taiwan or remote?

Created on May 21, 2026

2026   ·   quant-research   career   ·   projects

Intro

In today’s blog post, I will share how to find quantitative research opportunities in Taiwan or remotely. More specifically, I will focus on hedge funds such as Maven Security, Optiver, WorldQuant, and Point72; trading firms such as Kronos, A&R Research Capital, VICI Holdings, UC Capital, and Holdwin Capital; and cryptocurrency exchanges such as Binance, Crypto.com, and Bitget.

Since these roles are highly competitive, I recommend applying only if you meet the following prerequisites:

  1. Participation in an IOI, IMO, or IPhO selection and training camp.
  2. An ICPC regional silver medal or above; for top hedge funds, a gold medal is preferred.
  3. A first-author paper at a top conference, such as ICML, ICLR, or NeurIPS.
  4. A top-5 ranking in WolrdQuant IQC Taiwan.
  5. Practical trading experience with a Sharpe ratio above 1.5.
  6. Buy-side quantitative research internship or full-time experience, rather than sell-side experience.

Moreover, most of them are either computer science or finance students at NYCU or NTHU who have received an Academic Excellence Award, or students majoring in computer science, electrical engineering, mathematics, physics, or finance at NTU.

Once you meet the above requirements, we can dive into how to prepare for interviews and eventually secure offers. First, I will outline the main sections below:

  1. Which NYCU courses are essential for entering this field?
  2. How can you write a curriculum vitae that helps you pass the first stage?
  3. How should you prepare for technical interviews?
  4. How should you prepare for behavioral interviews?
  5. What useful tips should you keep in mind throughout the application process?

Essential courses at NYCU

We can divide the main skills required for QR roles into four areas: mathematics, coding, statistics, and finance, with finance being optional. I recommend taking the following courses at NYCU.

For mathematics courses, I suggest taking:

  1. Linear Algebra (I), taught by Ming-Cheng Shiue
  2. Probability, taught by Ping-Chun Hsieh
  3. Discrete Mathematics
  4. Introduction to Analysis (I)
  5. Introduction to Analysis (II)
  6. Differential Equations
  7. Partial Differential Equations
  8. Stochastic Processes
  9. Optimization Algorithms, taught by Ping-Chun Hsieh

For coding courses, I suggest taking:

  1. Introduction to Algorithms, taught by Kai-Chiang Wu
  2. Competitive Programming(I)
  3. Competitive Programming (II)
  4. Competitive Programming(III)
  5. Machine Learning
  6. Deep Learning
  7. Deep Learning Labs
  8. Reinforcement Learning, taught by Ping-Chun Hsieh

For statistics courses, I suggest taking:

  1. Statistics
  2. Advanced Statistics

For finance courses, I suggest taking the graduate-level versions rather than similar undergraduate courses:

  1. Futures and Options
  2. Investment

Also, an important mindset is that instead of simply focusing on the knowledge taught in class, you should think more about the intuition behind it. For example, why is the sample variance intuitively larger than the population variance?

Write a Resume

I have five tips, as shown below:

  1. Fit the job description: Only include relevant and important experiences on your resume.
  2. Use a template: A good template allows you to focus on the content of your resume without worrying too much about its appearance.
  3. Use quantitative and well-known metrics: These metrics allow companies to objectively understand your impact, such as PnL, Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown, ICPC medals, paper publications, etc.
  4. Use strong verbs: Strong verbs help HR quickly understand your contribution and impact.
  5. Use LLMs to polish your writing: This can help reduce grammar errors.

Using the five strategies above can not only help you present your overall strengths but also help HR quickly determine whether you are suitable for the role.

Prep for Technical Interviews

To be more specific, technical interviews can be divided into two parts: the math part and the coding part.

For the math part, I strongly recommend the book Practical Guide to Quantitative Finance Interviews, written by Xinfeng Zhou, especially Chapters 2 to 4. You can look for this book through libraries or other legitimate sources.

For the coding part, I strongly recommend practicing on Codeforces and AtCoder, since problems on these platforms emphasize a comprehensive understanding of data structures and algorithms rather than simply applying templates.

For more company-specific questions, you can search on online platforms such as Reddit and 1Point3Acres. This can be really helpful when you have no idea how to start your preparation.

Prep for Behavioral Interviews

While some people may argue that these kinds of interviews are not very important, I still suggest preparing for them. This is because behavioral interview preparation can help you better understand what you truly want and what kind of work environment would make you feel satisfied.

I have two tips:

  1. STAR method: A framework that helps you tell clear and engaging stories.
  2. Collect Behavioral Questions: Collect and practice common behavioral questions in advance.

After preparing, you will have a deeper understanding of your future career and its working style, which is vital for building a long-term career path.

Useful Tips

In the following section, I recommend three methods for finding jobs:

  1. Mass-applying
  2. Building your LinkedIn profile
  3. Following up

I will explain why I recommend these methods and what outcomes you can expect from using them.

Mass-applying

From an expected value perspective, if you want to receive more offers, you need to increase either:

  1. the number of jobs you apply for, or
  2. the probability of receiving an offer from each application.

By mass applying, you naturally increase the number of applications you submit. At the same time, you can also learn practical and general skills through the application and interview process, such as using the STAR method to answer behavioral questions. As a result, you may gradually improve your chances of receiving offers.

Therefore, I recommend this strategy as long as you can still maintain the same quality of preparation for each potential interview. In other words, mass applying is effective only if your probability of passing an interview does not decrease too much.

Build LinkedIn

The benefits are as follows:

  1. More job opportunities: One major benefit is that you may receive job opportunities from HR representatives or headhunters at other companies. This means your resume has a higher chance of being seen. Moreover, many overseas job opportunities are posted on LinkedIn rather than on platforms such as 1111, 104, or Yourator.
  2. A clearer understanding of job details: When you are unsure whether a job is suitable for you, you can send messages to people who are already working in that field. They are usually willing to share their experiences and answer your questions.
  3. A way to explore your dreams: When you feel confused about whether your career or life path is realistic, you can check whether someone else has taken a similar path before. If no one has, it may mean that you are a pioneer. Such a path may be full of thorns, but it may also lead to something meaningful.

Following Up

Following up shows that you are proactive, which is a valuable quality during the application process. Therefore, I recommend following up on your application progress about one to two weeks after an interview. It can also help you estimate your timeline more clearly and feel less nervous.

For example, you may feel confused in the following situations:

  1. Before an interview: What will the interview be about? Will it focus on technical problems, such as LeetCode-style questions or probability, or behavioral questions, such as why you want to apply to this company?
  2. After an interview: Will I have another interview? After learning from the interview experience, can I do further research or work on a project to show my growth mindset? Can I also discuss what I learned with others?

Conclusion

In general, pursuing quantitative research roles is a very challenging path. Before even receiving online assessments, we often need to graduate from prestigious schools and have competitive awards or achievements. It is not enough to be someone who only crams knowledge overnight.

After passing the resume screening stage and receiving an invitation to the next interview, you still have to compete with many excellent candidates, some of whom may even have publications in top conferences.

However, I strongly believe in the following quote:

It is not our abilities that show what we truly are… it is our choices. –”Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets”

Many of us are willing to tolerate volatility in order to pursue higher returns, just like seeking excess returns in the market. Many of us also want to pay fewer taxes to a government we consider inefficient. For example, in Taiwan, overseas income is taxed only when it exceeds NTD 7,500,000.

Many of us are not satisfied with a stable but unhealthy work style in Hsinchu Science Park companies. Therefore, choosing a quantitative trading-related job is not only a career pursuit but also a way to express our beliefs.
