# How to get Quantitative Research roles in Taiwan or remote?

# Intro

In today’s blog post, I will share how to find quantitative research opportunities in Taiwan or remotely. More specifically, I will focus on hedge funds such as [Maven Security](https://www.mavensecurities.com/), [Optiver](https://optiver.com/), [WorldQuant](https://www.worldquant.com/), and [Point72](https://point72.com/); trading firms such as [Kronos](https://www.kronosresearch.com/), [A&R Research Capital](https://anr.capital/), [VICI Holdings](https://www.viciholdings.com/), [UC Capital](https://www.uccapital.com.tw/), and [Holdwin Capital](https://www.holdwin.com.tw/); and cryptocurrency exchanges such as [Binance](https://www.binance.com/zh-TC), [Crypto.com](https://crypto.com/), and [Bitget](https://www.bitget.com/zh-TC).

Since these roles are highly competitive, I recommend applying only if you meet the following prerequisites:

1. Participation in an [IOI](https://ioinformatics.org/), [IMO](https://www.imo-official.org/), or [IPhO](https://ipho-unofficial.org/) selection and training camp.
2. An [ICPC](https://icpc.global/) regional silver medal or above; for top hedge funds, a gold medal is preferred.
3. A first-author paper at a top conference, such as [ICML](https://icml.cc/), [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/), or [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/).
4. A top-5 ranking in [WolrdQuant IQC](https://www.worldquant.com/brain/iqc/) Taiwan.
5. Practical trading experience with a Sharpe ratio above 1.5.
6. Buy-side quantitative research internship or full-time experience, rather than sell-side experience.

Moreover, most of them are either computer science or finance students at [NYCU](https://www.nycu.edu.tw/nycu/ch/index) or [NTHU](https://www.nthu.edu.tw/) who have received an Academic Excellence Award, or students majoring in computer science, electrical engineering, mathematics, physics, or finance at [NTU](https://www.ntu.edu.tw/).

Once you meet the above requirements, we can dive into how to prepare for interviews and eventually secure offers. First, I will outline the main sections below:

1. Which [NYCU](https://www.nycu.edu.tw/nycu/ch/index) courses are essential for entering this field?
2. How can you write a curriculum vitae that helps you pass the first stage?
3. How should you prepare for technical interviews?
4. How should you prepare for behavioral interviews?
5. What useful tips should you keep in mind throughout the application process?

# Write a **Resume**

I have five tips, as shown below:

1. Fit the job description: Only include relevant and important experiences on your resume.
2. Use a [template](https://github.com/posquit0/Awesome-CV): A good template allows you to focus on the content of your resume without worrying too much about its appearance.
3. Use quantitative and well-known metrics: These metrics allow companies to objectively understand your impact, such as PnL, Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown, ICPC medals, paper publications, etc.
4. Use strong verbs: Strong verbs help HR quickly understand your contribution and impact.
5. Use LLMs to polish your writing: This can help reduce grammar errors.

Using the five strategies above can not only help you present your overall strengths but also help HR quickly determine whether you are suitable for the role.

# Prep for Behavioral Interviews

While some people may argue that these kinds of interviews are not very important, I still suggest preparing for them. This is because behavioral interview preparation can help you better understand what you truly want and what kind of work environment would make you feel satisfied.

I have two tips:

1. [STAR method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situation,_task,_action,_result): A framework that helps you tell clear and engaging stories.
2. [Collect Behavioral Questions](https://hackmd.io/@6PPVul2mS7OX2GuGXlUCDA/behavior_questions): Collect and practice common behavioral questions in advance.

After preparing, you will have a deeper understanding of your future career and its working style, which is vital for building a long-term career path.

# Useful Tips

In the following section, I recommend three methods for finding jobs:

1. Mass-applying
2. Building your LinkedIn profile
3. Following up

I will explain why I recommend these methods and what outcomes you can expect from using them.

## **Mass-applying**

From an expected value perspective, if you want to receive more offers, you need to increase either:

1. the number of jobs you apply for, or
2. the probability of receiving an offer from each application.

By mass applying, you naturally increase the number of applications you submit. At the same time, you can also learn practical and general skills through the application and interview process, such as using the STAR method to answer behavioral questions. As a result, you may gradually improve your chances of receiving offers.

Therefore, I recommend this strategy as long as you can still maintain the same quality of preparation for each potential interview. In other words, mass applying is effective only if your probability of passing an interview does not decrease too much.

## Build LinkedIn

The benefits are as follows:

1. More job opportunities:
   One major benefit is that you may receive job opportunities from HR representatives or headhunters at other companies. This means your resume has a higher chance of being seen. Moreover, many overseas job opportunities are posted on LinkedIn rather than on platforms such as 1111, 104, or Yourator.
2. A clearer understanding of job details:
   When you are unsure whether a job is suitable for you, you can send messages to people who are already working in that field. They are usually willing to share their experiences and answer your questions.
3. A way to explore your dreams:
   When you feel confused about whether your career or life path is realistic, you can check whether someone else has taken a similar path before. If no one has, it may mean that you are a pioneer. Such a path may be full of thorns, but it may also lead to something meaningful.

## Following Up

Following up shows that you are proactive, which is a valuable quality during the application process. Therefore, I recommend following up on your application progress about one to two weeks after an interview. It can also help you estimate your timeline more clearly and feel less nervous.

For example, you may feel confused in the following situations:

1. Before an interview:
   What will the interview be about? Will it focus on technical problems, such as LeetCode-style questions or probability, or behavioral questions, such as why you want to apply to this company?
2. After an interview:
   Will I have another interview? After learning from the interview experience, can I do further research or work on a project to show my growth mindset? Can I also discuss what I learned with others?
